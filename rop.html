01 Rules of Procedure Two special formats

The All India Political Parties Meet is not a UN committee. There are no countries, no flags on the placard and no resolutions. You portray a real Indian political leader, argue your party's line, and fight for a joint communiqué everyone can sign.

02How a session runs

  1. 01
    Portfolios, not countries

    Every delegate represents a leader from an Indian political party. You speak as that person, carrying their party's positions, their record and their rivalries into the room.

  2. 02
    Sessions of deliberation

    Debate flows through moderated discussion on sub-topics set by the Moderator. Between them, informal huddles are where cross-party blocs are actually built.

  3. 03
    Points and interjections

    Sharp, short interjections replace formal points of order. Wit and political knowledge score as heavily as volume — the Meet is deliberately less rigid than a UN body.

  4. 04
    Documentation

    The Meet works toward a joint communiqué. Blocs that cannot sign it may publish their own notes of dissent, which are read into the record.

  5. 05
    The Press is watching

    The International Press covers the Meet live. Expect your best one-liner — or your worst slip — in the evening paper.

  6. 06
    Winning the room

    Awards weigh research, consistency of portrayal and your role in reaching consensus. Airtime alone does not decide it.

03Terms you will hear

Portfolio
The specific leader you have been allotted. Every argument you make should be defensible as theirs.
Sub-topic
A narrower question within the agenda, opened by the Moderator for a fixed stretch of debate.
Bloc
An informal grouping of parties working toward shared language in the communiqué. Cross-party blocs are encouraged.
Joint communiqué
The Meet's single working document — the agreed statement the room is trying to produce.
Note of dissent
A recorded objection filed by a delegate or bloc that will not sign part of the communiqué.
Portfolio break
Arguing your own views instead of your leader's. The one thing the format genuinely penalises.

A semi-crisis unlike anything else on the floor: general managers drafting from every era of basketball history to build the greatest roster of all time — then defending it under fire from the room and the crisis desk.

01The format at a glance

Committee
NBA All-Time Greats Draft
Wing
Senior · Grades 9–12
Represents
A franchise general manager — portfolios allotted by the Executive Board
Backbone
A snake-order draft of players from every era of NBA history
Crisis
Updates, injuries and scandals injected by the crisis desk between rounds
Documentation
Directives to the crisis desk and trade agreements between GMs
Scored on
Roster balance, quality of your defence on the floor, and adaptability
Agenda
Draft the greatest roster in basketball history — and defend every pick

02How a session runs

  1. 01
    You are a general manager

    Each delegate manages a franchise. Your job is to assemble the best possible roster from every player in NBA history — and to be able to argue for it.

  2. 02
    The draft

    Picks proceed in a snake order set by the Executive Board. Once a player is taken they are gone, so value your board before the round starts, not during it.

  3. 03
    Defend the pick

    After each round the floor opens. You justify your pick on era, statistics, fit and legacy — and you attack everyone else's on the same grounds.

  4. 04
    Trades and directives

    Between rounds, GMs may negotiate trades and send directives to the crisis desk. Injuries, scandals and twists arrive without warning.

  5. 05
    The verdict

    Rosters are judged by committee vote and Executive Board scoring across balance, argumentation and adaptability.

  6. 06
    Know the game

    Statistics win arguments, but stories win rooms. Eras, rivalries and championship pedigree all count when the vote comes.

03Terms you will hear

Snake order
The pick order reverses each round, so the last pick of one round also takes the first pick of the next.
Your board
Your private ranking of available players. Good GMs build one before the draft, not during it.
Defence round
The open floor after each round of picks, where every selection is justified and challenged.
Crisis update
An event announced by the crisis desk — an injury, a scandal, a rule change — that the committee must absorb as fact.
Directive
An instruction you send to the crisis desk, acting as your franchise, in response to events.
Ceiling
The peak quality of a roster, as opposed to its depth. The verdict weighs ceiling heavily.
