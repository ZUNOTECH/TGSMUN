AIPPM — How the Meet runs

The All India Political Parties Meet is not a UN committee: there are no countries, no placards with flags, and no resolutions. You portray a real Indian political leader, argue your party's line, and fight for a joint statement everyone can sign.

01Portfolios, not countries

Every delegate represents a leader from an Indian political party — you speak as that person, with their party's positions and rivalries.

02Sessions of deliberation

Debate flows through moderated discussion on sub-topics set by the Moderator, with informal huddles to build cross-party blocs.

03Points & interjections

Sharp, short interjections replace formal points — wit and political knowledge score as heavily as volume.

04Documentation

The Meet works toward a joint communiqué; dissenting blocs may publish their own notes of dissent.

05Press is watching

The International Press sits in — expect your best one-liner (or worst slip) in the evening paper.

06Winning the room

Awards weigh research, consistency of portrayal, and your role in the final consensus — not just airtime.

Q1In AIPPM, who do you represent?

AIPPM is portfolio-based — you speak as an actual leader, carrying their party's line into the Meet.

Q2What does the Meet work toward instead of a resolution?

The output document is a joint communiqué — with notes of dissent from blocs that refuse to sign.

Q3Can you contradict your leader's real party position?

Consistency of portrayal is scored — departures from the party line need political logic your leader could actually defend.

Q4What matters most for awards in AIPPM?

The gavel goes to delegates who know their politics, stay in character, and move the room toward agreement.

NBA All-Time Greats Draft — How it runs

A semi-crisis unlike anything else on the floor: general managers drafting from every era of basketball history to build the greatest roster of all time — then defending it under fire from the room and the crisis desk.

01You are a GM

Each delegate manages a franchise. Your job: assemble the best possible roster from every player in NBA history.

02The draft

Picks proceed in a snake order set by the Executive Board. Once a player is taken, they're gone — value your board.

03Defend the pick

After each round, the floor opens: you justify your pick on eras, stats, fit and legacy — and attack everyone else's.

04Trades & directives

Between rounds, GMs may negotiate trades and send directives to the crisis desk — injuries, scandals and twists arrive without warning.

05The verdict

Rosters are judged by committee vote and Executive Board scoring across balance, argumentation and adaptability.

06Know the game

Stats win arguments, but stories win rooms — eras, rivalries and championship DNA all count.

Test your understanding 0 / 4 correct

Q1What is your role in this committee?

Every delegate is a GM — the players are your draft pool, not your portfolio.

Q2A player you wanted was just drafted by another GM. What now?

Drafted players are gone. Great GMs re-rank on the fly and use the trade window between rounds.

Q3What makes it a *semi-crisis* committee?

The structured draft is the backbone; injuries, scandals and surprise events from the desk keep it live.

Q4How are rosters finally judged?

Balance, argumentation and adaptability are scored — a stat sheet alone won't carry a weak defence.
